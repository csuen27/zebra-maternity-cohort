# Transparent Data Sharing for ZEBRA Maternity Cohort-based Research
This repository provides an anonymised and de-identified, study-specific dataset limited to the variables necessary to reproduce and verify the analyses reported in the study “Maternal mental health liveability shaped by composite residential environmental profiles”, accepted for publication in Nature Cities. The repository will be actively maintained and updated in response to reasonable research requests for 12 months following publication. Researchers seeking further access to the ZEBRA maternity cohort should submit a brief research proposal to the principal investigators and corresponding authors, Haitong Zhe Sun or Hefeng Huang, for review. Requests will usually receive a response within four weeks. Following approval by the ZEBRA Collaborative Committee and the Health Commission of Zhejiang Province, restricted access can be granted for research collaboration purposes for reasons of participant confidentiality and data-sharing agreements with the Chinese Medical Association (CMA).

### General principles
Our collaborations are guided by a commitment to scientific discovery and to maximising the research value of the data collected. We operate on a non-commercial basis and, once a formal collaboration has been established, do not charge a separate fee for access to the ZEBRA database itself. However, to safeguard the intellectual property associated with the cohort, we require the primary institutional affiliations of the first author and the lead corresponding author on all resulting publications to be institutions within the ZEBRA Collaborative Network. 
 
We offer different collaboration pathways for independent principal investigators (PI) and for postdoctoral researchers or students, as outlined below. 
 
#### A) Postdoctoral researchers and students
We encourage postdoctoral researchers and students to consider joining the NUS Yong Loo Lin School of Medicine as visiting scholars or visiting students for a 12-month research placement. Applicants will be required to submit their supporting credentials and a two-to-four-page research proposal in English for review by the NUS academic committee. The necessary documentation will also be subject to review by the relevant Singapore authorities (like the Ministry of Manpower) for the issuance of a Training Employment Pass (TEP). 
 
Given the current level of competition, we strongly encourage applicants to seek external funding, such as support from the China Scholarship Council (CSC). Research would be conducted using NUS-registered computers equipped with institutional data protection safeguards. For work undertaken at NUS through this arrangement, NUS would normally be designated as the primary institutional affiliation, with the researcher’s home institution listed as a secondary affiliation in any resulting publications. 
 
#### B) Independent PIs
We particularly welcome independent PIs who can contribute complementary maternity or birth cohort resources to the ZEBRA Collaborative Network. Our existing cohort covers 15 provinces in China. If you have access to cohort resources from provinces beyond our current coverage, we would be pleased to explore a long-term partnership focused on expanding the network’s geographical coverage and research capacity. 
 
#### C) Institutional partnerships 
A further pathway is through a university-level agreement for joint training or a memorandum of understanding establishing long-term collaboration. We have reviewed the records held by the Dean’s Office and understand that your institution is not currently covered by such an arrangement.
